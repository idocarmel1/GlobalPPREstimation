# Pelagic and analogue membership reassessment — 30 September 2026

This review evaluates the **current adopted group set** for 43 exact catch labels. It recommends **19 raises and 24 retains, with no lowers**: nine Low → Medium, three Very low → Medium, six Very low → High, and Ruvettus pretiosus Very low → Low. The resulting distribution is **6 High, 12 Medium, 15 Low and 10 Very low**. Every retained decision has its own reason in [pelagic_review.json](pelagic_review.json). No mapping, numerical weight, canonical assumed flag, workbook, report or skill was changed.

The review read the original source-definition extract, the current M1–M11 guidance, taxonomy guidance, every assigned prior audit/ledger row, and regional catch reporting metadata. FAO taxonomic and fishery guides, taxonomic authorities and source studies supply the external biological evidence. Lengths in different metrics are kept distinct. Sources, sections and unresolved contradictions are recorded per taxon.

## Strongest revised decisions

- **Scomberomorus, Auxis, Scomberoides, Chirocentrus, Scomber, Hemiramphus and Thunnus** have sufficiently coherent regional size/habitat evidence for the combined large-pelagic pair. **Rachycentridae** is monotypic; its family rank is no reason to infer a diverse catch mixture.
- **Scombroidei** is interpreted through its actual reporting meaning. The raw SAU record calls it tunas, bonitos and billfishes; [FAO area-71 statistics](https://www.fao.org/4/y5852e/Y5852E12.htm) also use the taxon for tuna-like fishes, separately from hairtails. Medium is supported for this reporting category. It would be incorrect to silently substitute the full historical suborder and then penalize it for hypothetical hairtails.
- **Six exact billfish/swordfish species** rise from Very low to High under M3: Xiphias gladius, Istiophorus platypterus, Makaira mazara, Istiompax indica, Kajikia audax and Tetrapturus angustirostris. Their verified size and pelagic ecology satisfy the explicit generic definition with no competing source pool. Absence from a species list and shelf-model transfer are not membership ceilings. Istiophoridae, Istiophorus and Makaira retain their reviewed Medium broad-label decisions; this focused correction does not claim a new exhaustive historical reporting-scope audit for them. All nine remain Medium overall because their W4 stage weights are unchanged.
- **Ruvettus pretiosus** rises to Low because [FAO explicitly calls it benthopelagic](https://www.fao.org/docrep/pdf/009/t0539e/t0539e08.pdf), and its depth range overlaps the shelf edge. Final group 27 nevertheless has unresolved provenance relative to the appendix's two size pools.

## Unresolved conflicts and non-adopted proposals

1. **Decapterus maruadsi:** the adopted small-pelagic pool has a ≤30 cm maximum-TL definition. [Ohshimo et al. (2006)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/j.1444-2906.2006.01227.x) estimate an asymptotic FL of 34.2 cm; [Uehara et al. (2021), Table 3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsfo/85/3/85_153/_pdf) separately summarize a 35.4 cm observed maximum from that study. An asymptote is not itself an observed maximum, and the observation is being cited through a later table. These findings warrant checking the original record and a **separate proposal** for groups 30–31. Current membership remains Low. The thesis's use of this species to borrow mortality for both small and large pelagics is not a membership declaration.
2. **Seriola:** pelagic and epibenthic biology competes with the adopted exclusively pelagic pair. Review group 27 after its source aggregation is resolved.
3. **Selaroides leptolepis:** size fits small fish, but the [FAO account](https://www.fao.org/4/x2400e/x2400e55.pdf) describes demersal schools. Review group 24 versus 29; current membership remains Low.
4. **Lepidocybium flavobrunneum:** deep continental-slope association supports retaining Very low for the current pelagic analogue. Group 27 is a possible alternative, not an approved replacement.
5. **Lampris guttatus:** retain Very low provisionally for the historical deep-water opah label. Its [taxonomic revision](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29690102/) prevents importing one newly delimited species as the identity of all historical catch. This is the most debatable retained analogue: the source does not explicitly exclude Lampris. The conservative rating reflects uncertainty that the deep-water assemblage is represented, and should not be cited as a demonstrated prohibition in the generic pelagic definition.
6. **Rastrelliger/Scombridae:** the recovered R. faughni entry gives an incomplete small-size statement, not a reliable upper bound. Do not claim proven ≤30 cm from “at least” wording, or from a small research sample. Whole-label large-pelagic containment remains insufficiently supported.
7. **Broad unidentified categories:** the candidate sets are reasonable approximations, but specific reporting boundaries remain unresolved (shark inclusion, named mixed-habitat pools, or pelagic invertebrates). Their Very low ratings are not justified merely by uncertain allocation shares. **Chondrichthyes** explicitly includes chimaeras, whereas the source pools are elasmobranchs. **Gadiformes** includes small pelagic codlets as well as benthopelagic taxa, so its retained Very low is better expressed as M10 broad containment uncertainty than as a blanket deep-water M11 claim.

No proposed group correction is incorporated in the reassessment. The separate parent review owns any future mapping decision.

## Reusable lessons

- Read the **operative group definition**, not just its short label. Resolve an apparent source typo from the heading, examples and linked stanzas, document the interpretation, and preserve the discrepancy.
- Test taxon identity, all size/habitat predicates, dedicated-group exclusions, then stage eligibility. A genus or family can be coherent enough for Medium. Conversely, one incompatible regional species is enough to defeat universal containment, but is not evidence of its abundance in the catch.
- Separate **species maximum size** from caught individual length, maturity and age. Juveniles of large species remain within that species' large-fish stage pair; small species do not become large-species juveniles.
- Keep TL, FL and SL explicit. A measured FL or SL already exceeding 30 cm establishes TL above the threshold. A value below the threshold in a shorter metric does not establish maximum TL ≤30 cm. Avoid substituting growth asymptotes for observed maxima.
- A complete explicit generic definition can support **M3 High** without naming the taxon. Use M5/M9 only when a real membership assumption remains; an ecological evidence source, a verified synonym, missing species enumeration or an assumed stage ratio is not by itself a Medium membership ceiling. Taxonomic disputes that leave all relevant guild predicates unchanged need not reduce membership.
- Separate **candidate-set membership** from allocation. The original thesis assumes approximately 90% juvenile catch for the large-pelagic group. The derived 89.993906%/10.006094% model ratio is numerically reproducible, but remains an assumed composition for another taxon/year/gear.
- Reporting labels can be narrower than their apparent scientific taxon. Use raw common names, commercial classes and documented statistical conventions together. These establish interpretation, not measured species proportions. Never turn an averaged database size field into a species maximum.
- Separate model geography and parameter transfer from functional membership. Oceanic distribution does not automatically negate a generic pelagic guild. Conversely, bottom association, a missing deep-water assemblage, or an unresolved final pool can be substantive conflicts. State which issue actually limits a row.
- Do not quietly modernize a historical taxon. Verified synonym chains identify the evidence; they do not prove source-explicit membership. Contested names such as Makaira mazara should stay contested. Historical Carangoides and Lampris labels may retain broader reporting scope.
- Common names are not family definitions. Bramidae “pomfrets” do not inherit the source's explicitly Stromateidae group.
- Broad categories need explicit inclusion/exclusion reasoning. Include source named groups when their taxa are eligible; do not default to residual pools. An all-fish candidate set can be useful while still leaving reporting scope unresolved. Caught Acetes within Zooplankton shows why “invertebrates” cannot automatically mean benthic pools alone.
- A confidence-only review must evaluate the adopted mapping before proposing a better one. Keep group corrections separately marked as not adopted; do not rate the proposed mapping as if it were current.

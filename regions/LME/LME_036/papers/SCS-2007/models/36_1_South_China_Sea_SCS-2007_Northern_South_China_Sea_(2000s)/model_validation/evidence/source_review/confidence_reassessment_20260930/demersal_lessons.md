# Demersal and mixed-label membership confidence review

Reviewed 30 September 2026. The accompanying `demersal_review.json` contains one decision for each of the 85 exact input labels. This is a confidence reassessment of the existing assignments. No group membership, catch, numerical parameter, stage weight, or canonical mapping was changed.

| Result | Labels |
|---|---:|
| Raise Low to High | 15 |
| Raise Low to Medium | 21 |
| Retain Low with a specific reason | 49 |
| Lower | 0 |
| Total | 85 |

Four High decisions are **Miichthys miiuy, Atrobucca nibe, Nibea albiflora, and Nibea mitsukurii**, whose Sciaenidae membership and maxima above 30 cm meet the explicit large-croaker definition. Eleven additional identified species have directly documented demersal ecology, a maximum above 30 cm, applicable regional occurrence and no competing dedicated pool; they meet the complete generic definition under M3. A species need not be enumerated by the author to earn High.

The remaining 21 improvements are Medium: 11 require a stated ecological interpretation (reef/inshore inclusion, discordant habitat/depth metadata, or the final benthopelagic boundary), and 10 use a supported assumed regional eligible set under M9. The approved model-catch weights remain a separate Medium component, so High membership does not make an age-split assignment High overall.

## Reusable distinctions

**Apply M3 to verified generic criteria as well as named taxonomic pools.** Missing species enumeration is not itself uncertainty. Reserve M5 for a real boundary interpretation or ecological extension, and record that assumption explicitly.

1. **Assess the union before its proportions.** Appendix 6.1.20 says adult large demersal fish are older than 18 months, but also describes juveniles as below age 2. The unresolved 18–24-month convention is real. It does not exclude a qualifying species from the union of groups 25 and 26. Unknown landed ages similarly do not establish Low membership. Leave the approved model-catch allocation at its separate confidence unless the allocation review finds a different issue.

2. **The threshold is maximum species total length.** It is not common length, maturity length, a juvenile's size, or the largest individual in a particular catch. Pomadasys argenteus has a common length of 25 cm TL but a maximum of 70 cm TL; Parupeneus barberinus has a common length of 30 cm TL but a maximum of 60 cm TL. Both support the large demersal pair. Conversely, a species with a maximum exactly 30 cm TL belongs on the source's inclusive small side. ([P. argenteus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Pomadasys-argenteus.html), [P. barberinus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Parupeneus-barberinus.html))

3. **Preserve length metrics.** A maximum SL or FL above 30 cm establishes that TL exceeds 30 cm; it does not require treating the measures as equal. A value below 30 cm SL or FL cannot automatically establish TL at or below 30 cm. Ariomma indica remains Low partly because its available maximum is 25 cm **SL**, near the relevant boundary, and final group 27's size scope is unresolved. Do not convert an unspecified length code into TL. ([FAO Ariommatidae](https://www.fao.org/4/y0870e/y0870e42.pdf))

4. **A broad rank is not an automatic downgrade.** Paralichthyidae, Bothidae, Pleuronectiformes and Cynoglossus already map to small plus juvenile/adult large demersal groups. Their documented size diversity supports that set; unknown component fractions belong to allocation. Conversely, Ariidae, Mullidae, Platycephalidae, Terapon, Sillago and several reef-fish families have specific small-species counterexamples to their current large-only set. Those remain Low because a candidate is missing, not because they are families or genera.

5. **Test regional scope before using a counterexample.** The FAO Western Central Pacific guide recognizes only Scatophagus argus within the regional genus. A small Scatophagus species from another region is not enough to overturn that regional interpretation. Scatophagus is raised to Medium. Psettodidae is the converse: FAO identifies only the large Psettodes erumei in the region, so its current inclusion of small group 24 needs review. ([FAO Scatophagidae, p.3623](https://www.fao.org/4/y0870e/y0870e26.pdf), [FAO Psettodidae, pp.3792–3793](https://www.fao.org/docrep/pdf/009/y0870e/y0870e43.pdf))

6. **Use habitat behavior, not depth or a single coarse tag.** Reef-associated Platycephalus indicus and Lethrinus miniatus have direct bottom-use evidence. Bathydemersal does not mean pelagic. At the same time, feeding on benthic organisms does not prove exclusively demersal residence: Aluterus monoceros has explicit benthopelagic and surface-association evidence. Leiognathidae combines bottom-living species with some deeper nocturnal midwater migrants, leaving a real 24-versus-27 composition question. ([P. indicus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Platycephalus-indicus.html), [L. miniatus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Lethrinus-miniatus.html), [A. monoceros](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Aluterus-monoceros.html), [FAO Leiognathidae](https://www.fao.org/4/y0770e/y0770e03.pdf))

7. **Dedicated taxonomy overrides common names and generic habitat.** Large Nibea albiflora remains a dedicated large croaker despite a benthopelagic label. Sparid “breams” are not Nemipteridae, Pagrus “snappers” are not Lutjanidae, Polynemidae threadfins are not threadfin breams, and Parastromateus niger is Carangidae rather than Stromateidae. A common “pomfret” name does not establish the Psenopsis anomala pool.

8. **Reconcile the taxonomy used by the model.** Modern sources may put traditional Caesionidae within Lutjanidae as Caesioninae. That does not demonstrate that the 2007 author included fusiliers in the dedicated snapper group. Caesio/Caesionidae remain Low for documented midwater ecology and unresolved source-era scope; no silent dedicated-group extension was made. ([FAO fusilier catalogue](https://www.fao.org/4/t0046e/t0046e00.htm), [current Caesio cuning account](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Caesio-cuning.html))

## Final benthopelagic group: numerical cross-check

The selected model has one group 27, “Benthopelagic fish.” Appendix 6.1.21–6.1.22 separately describes small and large species. The final label alone neither proves a merge nor proves that one component was excluded.

| Quantity | Final model/table | Appendix small | Appendix large |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2000s P/B | 3.08 | 3.08 | 1.91 |
| 2000s Q/B | 15.42 | approximately 15.4 from P/Q = 0.2 | empirical estimate, not matched here |
| 2000s catch | 0.6025 in model; 0.603 rounded in Table 6.2 | 0.643 | 0.0079 |
| 1970s P/B | 3.00 | 2.31 | 1.29 |
| 1970s catch | 0.0303 | 0.0023 | 0.0053 |

The final 2000s P/B and Q/B closely follow the small component. Final catch is neither the small value, the large value, nor their sum (0.6509). The 1970s values likewise do not reproduce either component or their catch sum (0.0076). Chapter 6 notes changes during balancing, so these comparisons do not identify a unique merger operation. Final group 27 has B = 0.922 and EE = 0.479; no separate final large-benthopelagic row was found.

**Decision:** small Mene maculata and its monotypic family Menidae have enough independent size/habitat evidence for Medium ecological membership in 27. Large benthopelagic Parastromateus, Mugil cephalus, Lobotes and Beryx-related labels remain Low because the appendix large component's final representation is unverified. Ariomma also retains the length-metric issue. Do not call final 27 definitively “small only,” and do not call the appendix groups a documented deliberate merge.

Primary local evidence: Cheung thesis, Tables 6.1–6.2 (pp.175–179), balancing discussion, Appendix 6.1.21–6.1.22 (pp.337–338), and selected `model.json` group 27. Exact repository paths and per-row evidence are retained in the JSON.

## Candidate-set proposals and limits

All 49 retained-Low rows contain a separately marked **nonadopted scientific proposal**. Some request a candidate-set correction; others request evidence resolving the current group's scope. They are not approved replacements or computed allocations.

Priority concrete cases include:
- **Siganus canaliculatus:** current group 24 conflicts with the reviewed maximum 40 cm TL; an older guide reports 30 cm. Preserve both records and reconcile maximum-size evidence before a change to 25+26. The discrepancy is not resolved by assuming that landed fish are smaller. ([current account](https://fishbase.se/Fieldguide/FieldGuideSummary.php?c_code=608&genusname=Siganus&speciesname=canaliculatus), [older FAO guide](https://www.fao.org/4/r7066e/r7066e21.pdf))
- **Psettodidae:** review removal of 24 because regional membership is the large P. erumei.
- **Families/genera spanning size classes:** review adding 24 only where identified regional small species support it. Do not infer their catch fractions from existence alone.
- **Drepane and Mugil:** reconcile genus-versus-species habitat assignments rather than assigning different pools without a composition explanation.
- **Chirocentrus dorab:** FAO explicitly describes coastal pelagic behavior, supporting review of large-pelagic 30+31 versus current 27. ([FAO account, p.202](https://www.fao.org/4/an020e/an020e.pdf))

Species-range or name caveats (notably Nibea mitsukurii, Lateolabrax japonicus and the Mugil cephalus complex) remain visible. The review treats the supplied catch labels as the objects being assigned; it does not correct catch identity, claim measured local species composition, or remove the existing whole-LME/year transfer assumption.

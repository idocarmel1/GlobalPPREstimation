# Trophodynamics of marine organisms in the epipelagic layer of the Okhotsk Sea in the 2000s
K. M. Gorbatenko and I. V. Melnikov
Pacific Branch of VNIRO (TINRO), 4 Shevchenko Alley, Vladivostok 690091, Russia.
Izvestiya TINRO, 2019, volume 198, pp. 143-163. UDC 574.523(265.53).
DOI: 10.26428/1606-9919-2019-198-143-163.
## Abstract translated from Russian
Trophodynamic modeling provided new data on the transfer of matter and energy among the principal biotic components of the Okhotsk Sea ecosystem, taking into account their rates of production and food consumption. Research data on zooplankton and nekton, the feeding of aquatic organisms, isotope composition, and organic carbon made it possible to substantiate the principal trophodynamic relationships in pelagic and bottom communities of the Okhotsk Sea for 2000-2014. Total zooplankton production for the entire sea was 2616 million tonnes wet weight (non-predatory plankton, 2275 million tonnes; predatory plankton, 341 million tonnes). In addition to favorable climatic and oceanographic conditions, the high total zooplankton production of the Okhotsk Sea is associated with the dominance of highly productive species. Calculations of zooplankton consumption by predators showed that only 22.4% of total annual zooplankton production is consumed during the year (predatory plankton, 16.2%; nekton, 6.2%). Using organic carbon as a measure of energy made it possible to establish that annual organic matter production in the Okhotsk Sea pelagic zone is 831.0 million tC at trophic level I, 177.400 at II, 18.100 at III, 0.740 at IV, and 0.016 million tC/year at V. Comparing production and consumption levels indicates that pelagic nekton consumed an average of 159 million tonnes of food per year in the 2000s (2000-2014). In the annual nekton diet, zooplankton accounted for 85.5%, nekton for 12.8%, and zoobenthos for 1.7% by biomass. Pollock accounted for 50.7% of total nekton food consumption in the Okhotsk Sea epipelagic layer; herring, 18.9%; squid, 16.6%; capelin, 7.6%; deep-sea smelt, 5.3%; and salmon, 0.9%. In total, nekton consumes only 6.2% of annual zooplankton production. Total annual production of aquatic organisms in the epipelagic layer exceeds 1 billion tC (1027.4 million tC/year, or 17.85 billion tonnes wet weight). Excluding primary production, which constitutes 67.60% of gross production in carbon equivalents, the largest contributions to production are made by microheterotrophs, 13.30%, and the dominant zooplankton groups (copepods, 11.40%; euphausiids, 5.50%; sagittas, 1.20%; hyperiids, 0.50%); nekton accounts for only 0.13%.
Keywords: trophodynamic relationships; epipelagic layer; zooplankton; nekton; Okhotsk Sea.

## Introduction
The Okhotsk Sea is Russia's principal fishing region, and the condition of its biological resources has been monitored regularly for many decades [Shuntov, 2016]. The study of the food supply and trophic relationships of aquatic organisms has almost always formed part of this research. With the practical introduction of ecological monitoring and ecosystem approaches, trophological research has needed to expand and deepen, including clarification of the trophic status and energetic value of aquatic organisms.
One of the principal tasks in research on the trophodynamics of aquatic organisms is to establish the patterns of biological transformation of matter and energy in water bodies. In aquatic ecosystems, identifying discrete trophic levels between which matter and energy are transferred is difficult because a substantial part of plant and animal production accumulates as detritus. This is consumed by many aquatic species, particularly invertebrates, a large proportion of which are omnivorous. Identifying and assessing energy flows in water bodies therefore requires determination of the actual trophic status and energy potential of all abundant species constituting the food web.
Until now, the study of aquatic ecosystems through analysis of natural stable-isotope ratios and determination of the quantity of organic carbon in aquatic organisms has not been widely used in Russian hydrobiological research. Given the importance of this problem, such studies were initiated at TINRO-Center in 2003 [Gorbatenko et al., 2004a].
One direction of trophological research is ecosystem modeling, which is becoming increasingly developed in studies of many regions of the World Ocean, including the Far Eastern seas. Trophodynamic models previously constructed for these seas [Dulepova, 2002; Radchenko, 2011, 2015; Zavolokin, 2014] made it possible, at a certain stage, to investigate ecosystem process dynamics and their balance. The models presented differed substantially...
@@145
...because complete information on the abundance of all ecosystem components, particularly those at the lower trophic levels, and their roles in trophic flows was lacking [Zavolokin, 2014], as were reliable energy indices for aquatic organisms expressed in calories or organic carbon. Earlier, in constructing trophodynamic models, V. I. Radchenko [2011] noted that at the present stage of research on trophic relationships of Okhotsk Sea plankton, we cannot provide reliable information on diets or determine what proportions of the diets of individual species, or even groups of plankton, consist of autotrophs and heterotrophs.
There are several approaches to determining primary production and energy transformation in food webs, but modern studies most often express quantitative indices in units of carbon mass. Carbon is the principal element of the organic matter transformed in ecosystems and occurs in every component of organisms: fats, proteins, and carbohydrates. Studies of the formation of organic carbon and its transformation pathways require its quantity in aquatic organisms to be determined so that subsequent matter or energy flows in the food web can be expressed in units of carbon mass. At present, the chemical composition of organisms is most easily and accurately determined by combustion in an elemental analyzer (a CN analyzer).
This study uses information obtained during TINRO-Center expeditions in the 2000s that investigated the Okhotsk Sea epipelagic layer: the composition, structure, and interannual dynamics of plankton and nekton communities; the feeding of aquatic organisms; the isotope composition of soft tissues; and the quantity of organic carbon in dominant populations.
The principal objective is a quantitative assessment of the trophodynamics of pelagic aquatic organisms in the epipelagic layer of the Okhotsk Sea.
## Materials and methods
Plankton was sampled in the 0-200 m layer (surface to bottom at depths below 200 m) with a BSD net (mouth area 0.1 m², mesh size 0.168 mm), and processed using the standard methodology adopted at TINRO-Center, incorporating corrections for under-sampling of individual plankton groups [Volkov, 2008]. More than 10,000 plankton samples were collected and processed.
Daily, seasonal, and annual food consumption had previously been determined for various planktonic and nektonic organisms forming the basis of pelagic biocoenoses; the materials and methods have already been described in detail in the literature [Gorbatenko et al., 2004a, b; Gorbatenko, 2016a, b; Gorbatenko and Levitskaya, 2016; Gorbatenko and Melnikov, 2016].
Zooplankton and nekton were collected for isotope analysis and organic carbon determination in 2003-2014. Net plankton was collected with Bongo, BSD, and IKS plankton nets. Plankton stations and trawl stations coincided (Fig. 1). In most investigations of the pelagic zone, an 80/376 midwater trawl with a small-mesh liner was used.
The ratios of stable carbon isotopes (¹³C and ¹²C) and nitrogen isotopes (¹⁵N and ¹⁴N) in the samples were determined in the conventional form as the relative quantity δ, in per mille, measured as the difference between the isotope ratio in the sample and in a standard:
!EQ|For carbon: δ¹³C (‰) = [(¹³C/¹²C_sample - ¹³C/¹²C_standard)/(¹³C/¹²C_standard)] × 1000.
!EQ|For nitrogen: δ¹⁵N (‰) = [(¹⁵N/¹⁴N_sample - ¹⁵N/¹⁴N_standard)/(¹⁵N/¹⁴N_standard)] × 1000.
To standardize the data, stable carbon isotope values are expressed relative to Vienna Pee Dee Belemnite carbonate (V-PDB), and nitrogen values relative to atmospheric nitrogen. Calibration used IAEA CH-6, NBS-22, IAEA N-1, and IAEA N-2 standards (International Atomic Energy Agency, Vienna). The precision of δ¹³C and δ¹⁵N determinations was ±0.06 and ±0.10‰, respectively.

!FIG|1
The actual trophic level occupied by a species in aquatic food webs was determined from nitrogen isotope composition [Post, 2002]:
!EQ|TL_c = λ + (δ¹⁵N_c - δ¹⁵N_b)/Δ.
Here TL_c is the trophic level of the consumer; δ¹⁵N_c is the nitrogen isotope composition of the consumer; δ¹⁵N_b is the nitrogen isotope composition of organisms used as the base of the food chain (primary producers or primary consumers); Δ is the change in isotope composition over one trophic level, taken as 3.4‰ [Minagawa and Wada, 1984]; and λ is the trophic level of the organisms taken as the base of the food chain (I for primary producers or II for primary consumers).
Because direct measurements of the isotope composition of phytoplankton were unavailable, the copepod Eucalanus bungii, a first-order consumer, was used as the base of the pelagic food chain. This copepod species, a fine-particle filter feeder, showed the lowest δ¹⁵N values among Okhotsk Sea zooplankton in every season in our data. We therefore consider it to occupy trophic level II [Gorbatenko et al., 2014]. We also adopted δ¹⁵N_b values of 7.8 in spring, 6.9 in summer, 7.8 in autumn, and an overall seasonal mean of 7.4.
To determine organic carbon and nitrogen, samples of individual aquatic species consisted of 1-100 individuals, depending on organism size. For nekton, 3-10 g of muscle tissue was taken from the dorsal side, rinsed with distilled water to remove salts, and dried in an oven for 12-24 hours at 60°C until fully dehydrated. The dried samples were ground with a corundum mortar and pestle, redried to constant mass, and used to determine organic carbon and nitrogen by combustion in a EURO-3000 elemental CNH analyzer.
A review of production calculation methods showed that all are based on data on somatic and reproductive growth of the individuals constituting a population [Zaika, 1972; Sushchenya, 1975; Vinberg, 1979; Ivanova, 1985; and others]. Determining population production requires information on the rate of production by individuals, that is, growth rate,...
@@147
...the formation of reproductive products and exuviae, and the excretion of liquid metabolites [Ivanova, 1985]. Data on age composition, biomass, and density were used to calculate the specific daily production of aquatic organisms. Copepod production was calculated conventionally using the Boysen-Jensen equation [Boysen-Jensen, 1919]:
!EQ|P_t = B_2 - B_1 + B_e.
Here B_1 and B_2 are species biomass at the beginning and end of the observation period t = t_2 - t_1; B_e is the loss due to grazing, natural mortality, and losses of matter during life. B_e was calculated from:
!EQ|B_e = N_1 - N_2 (1/2)(B_1/N_1 + B_2/N_2).
Here N_1 and N_2 are the initial and final abundance, respectively, over a specified period.
!NOTE|The grouping of N_1 and N_2 in this displayed equation follows the printed source. No missing parentheses have been supplied.
Production of systems above the population level, comprising several trophic levels, was determined using conventional methods [Zaika, 1983; Ivanova, 1985; Vinogradov and Shushkina, 1987]. Production at a trophic level equals the sum of production by its constituent populations. Production of a system consisting of n trophic levels is:
!EQ|P = P_1 + P_2 + ... + P_n - A_2 - ... - A_n.
Here P_n and A_n are the production and assimilated energy, respectively, at trophic level n.
Total production of a zooplankton community comprises the summed production of non-predatory zooplankton (phytophagous and euryphagous forms) and predatory zooplankton, minus the food assimilated by predators [Zaika, 1983; Ivanova, 1985]. Total zooplankton community production is determined by:
!EQ|P(zooplankton) = P(non-predatory zooplankton) + P(predatory zooplankton) - P(predatory zooplankton)/K.
K is the second-order efficiency coefficient for predatory plankton. According to Yu. I. Sorokin [1982], K for predatory plankton in marine ecosystems is 0.3-0.4. Total production indicates the overall volume of organic matter generated by zooplankton. Hydrobiological studies assessing food resources also use the actual production of zooplankton, calculated as the difference between the summed production of all community components and the part consumed within the community by planktonic predators. Actual production is thus the portion of total production available to fish as food.
Production characteristics of Okhotsk Sea zooplankton had previously been studied in dominant species [Shebanova, 2007; Shebanova et al., 2010, 2011, 2012; Chuchukalo et al., 2013]. The data in these studies made it possible to calculate the production of abundant species forming the biomass basis of the four dominant taxonomic groups of Okhotsk Sea zooplankton.
## Results and discussion
### Plankton
On the basis of published data [Shushkina and Vinogradov, 1988; Sorokin et al., 1995, 1997; Naletova et al., 1997; Matveev, 2006; Matveev and Zhigalov, 2008], obtained principally during academic research, we presently adopt Okhotsk Sea primary production, including recycling, as 450 gC/m²/year, bacterial production as 67.5 gC/m²/year, and protozoan production as 27.0 gC/m²/year. Together with phytoplankton, the microheterotrophs comprising suspended organic matter form the marine foundation on which all trophic pyramids are based [Sorokin, 1977].
Zooplankton studies at TINRO-Center over a 30-year period showed that five species dominated throughout the Okhotsk Sea all year. These are the interzonal...

...copepods Metridia okhotensis and Neocalanus plumchrus, the euphausiids Thysanoessa raschii and Thysanoessa longipes, and the sagitta Sagitta elegans [Gorbatenko, 1997]. Calculations of mean zooplankton biomass in the 2000s produced values close to the long-term means for the entire study period (Fig. 2). Long-term mean zooplankton biomass in the epipelagic layer during the 2000s is estimated at 214.6 million tonnes wet weight, or 140.9 g/m². Copepods account for 48.3% of biomass; euphausiids, 26.9%; sagittas, 19.9%; and amphipods, 2.7%.

Compared with the NWP, where Euphausia pacifica dominates with an annual P/B ratio of 6, euphausiid production in the Okhotsk Sea is more than 1.5 times that in adjacent Pacific waters. Among hyperiids, the most productive species, Themisto pacifica, also dominates in the Okhotsk Sea [Shebanova et al., 2014]. Thus the Okhotsk Sea can be considered the most productive water body because it is dominated by zooplankton species with maximal production.
Total epipelagic zooplankton production in the 2000s was estimated at 392.9 million tonnes in winter (P/B = 2.1), 666.8 in spring (P/B = 2.8), 1060.9 in summer (P/B = 4.2), and 495.2 million tonnes in autumn (P/B = 2.7) (Table 1).
Within zooplankton communities, low-trophic-status plankters, phytophages and euryphages, play the principal role in generating organic matter. The role of planktonic predators is also very substantial: on the one hand they generate organic matter, and on the other they consume filter feeders and reduce their production. Mean total annual zooplankton production for the entire sea in the 2000s was estimated at 2616 million tonnes wet weight, including 2275 million tonnes of non-predatory plankton, mainly copepods and euphausiids, and 341 million tonnes of predatory plankton, mainly sagittas and hyperiids.
### Feeding
Food composition and daily ration data for non-predatory and predatory plankton [Gorbatenko, 2016a, b] and nekton [Gorbatenko and Levitskaya, 2016; Gorbatenko and Melnikov, 2016] in the Okhotsk Sea during the 2000s were used to calculate prey consumption in different seasons and throughout the year.
Feeding data for predatory zooplankton, principally sagittas [Gorbatenko, 2016b], showed that predatory plankton consumes a total of 424 million tonnes annually. This is about 50.5% of the gross stock, or 16.2% of zooplankton production. The principal pressure falls on copepod populations: 19.7% of that group's total annual production (Table 2).

Pollock trophic level changes quite substantially with growth (Fig. 6): δ¹⁵N data show a clear relationship between changes in the trophic status of differently sized individuals and the proportions of plankton, benthos, and nekton in their diets [Gorbatenko and Levitskaya, 2016]. A similar lifetime shift to higher trophic levels is characteristic of many aquatic organisms, though the strength of this tendency varies. Unlike pollock, for example, the diet of Okhotsk Sea herring is based on two plankton groups, euphausiids and copepods, belonging to trophic level II and, to a lesser extent, III [Gorbatenko and Melnikov, 2016]. Elemental composition studies indicate that organic carbon content in the wet weight of crustaceans (copepods, euphausiids, mysids, and hyperiids) is twice that of Oikopleura and sagittas, and more than an order of magnitude higher than in jellyfish (Fig. 7). A single biomass-to-carbon conversion factor should therefore not be used for all zooplankton in production calculations, as in some studies [Dvoretsky and Dvoretsky, 2010; and others], because zooplankton carbon content depends directly on the dominance of a particular group or its constituent species.

### Herring
Herring predominated among nekton species at trophic level III; its average Okhotsk Sea stock in 2000-2014 was about 2.5 million tonnes [Gorbatenko and Melnikov, 2016]. V. I. Radchenko [1994] gives a mean herring P/B ratio of 0.34. Herring production is estimated at 0.86 million tonnes wet weight/year. Using data on nekton organic carbon, herring production is 0.119 million tC/year, or 0.7% of trophic level III in carbon equivalents (Table 3). Our studies showed that long-term mean total annual prey consumption by herring was 36.9 million tonnes [Gorbatenko and Melnikov, 2016]. Zooplankton predominated in the annual diet: 35.9 million tonnes, or 97.3% by biomass (4.2% of gross zooplankton stock).
### Salmon
Pacific salmon inhabit the Okhotsk Sea for only part of the year. Long-term mean adult salmon biomass in summer 2000-2014 was 483.2 thousand tonnes; autumn biomass of juvenile and adult salmon was determined as 272.4 thousand tonnes (unpublished data of A. N. Starovoitov). The most abundant salmon species in the Far Eastern seas is pink salmon Oncorhynchus gorbuscha, which has the highest production potential among salmon. According to V. I. Radchenko [1994], the mean annual P/B ratio of pink salmon is 7.70, whereas that of chum salmon of different ages is 0.84. The average P/B ratio for differently sized pink, chum (O. keta), and sockeye (O. nerka) salmon in the Okhotsk Sea was 1.34; these species account for 98.6% of all salmon in the sea. Calculations showed that their total production in the Okhotsk Sea was 0.105 million tC/year, or 0.6% of total trophic level III production in carbon equivalents (Table 3).
### Squid
Macroplankton is particularly important in the diet of small and medium-sized squid, while larger adults mainly eat nekton. Our data put total squid prey consumption at 32.4 million tonnes/year, with plankton averaging 64% of the diet and nekton 36%. Isotope studies place Onychoteuthis borealijaponica, Berrytheuthis magister, and Gonatopsis borealis at trophic level III; Gonatus madokai, Gonatus onyx, and others are at IV. Epipelagic squid grow rapidly, with annual P/B ratios estimated at 2.5-3.7, averaging 3.1 [Zuev et al., 1985; Nesis, 1985]. In 1979-2009, long-term mean nekton biomass in the Okhotsk Sea was estimated at 17.254 million tonnes, of which only about 2.0 million tonnes (1.2%) was squid [Shuntov and Temnykh, 2011]. Overall, cephalopod biomass in the Okhotsk Sea reaches 3 million tonnes and is concentrated principally in the bathypelagic and mesopelagic zones [Lapko, 1996]. As active migrants, squid ascend into the epipelagic layer to feed, and abundance there is particularly high at night. For calculating squid production in the epipelagic layer, we adopted a biomass of 1 million tonnes because these nekton organisms form concentrations in the epipelagic layer only at night. The mean P/B ratio for all squid is 2.5. Taking into account their organic carbon content, the lowest value among the nekton groups studied, their production is 0.105 million tC/year and their share of total trophic level III production is 0.6% in carbon equivalents (Table 3).
### Deep-sea smelt
Eight mesopelagic fish species have been recorded in the Okhotsk Sea epipelagic layer, with deep-sea smelt predominating. Despite its high gross biomass, averaging 1.8 million tonnes annually, deep-sea smelt production at trophic level III does not exceed 0.06 million tC/year, or 0.3% in carbon equivalents (Table 3). Our data put its long-term mean total prey consumption at 10.29 million tonnes/year (see Fig. 3), or 5.3% of total nekton prey consumption. Euphausiids, 46.1%, and copepods, 40.7%, predominate in its annual diet.

### Juvenile pollock (<30 cm)
Pollock up to 30 cm long are typical planktivores [Gorbatenko, 1997], and their Okhotsk Sea biomass in 2000-2014 was comparable with salmon and capelin, averaging 0.8 million tonnes [Ovsyannikov et al., 2013]. V. I. Radchenko [2015] gives a mean juvenile pollock P/B ratio of 2.5, close to that of squid. Juvenile pollock production is estimated at 2 million tonnes wet weight/year. Taking organic carbon content into account, production is 0.197 million tC/year, or 1.1% of trophic level III in carbon equivalents (Table 3). Thus, because of their high production, juvenile pollock make the largest contribution to total nekton production at trophic level III. Our data put their long-term mean total annual prey consumption at 16.5 million tonnes. Zooplankton predominates in the annual diet, 98.9% by biomass.
At trophic level IV in the Okhotsk Sea are differently sized pollock, 30-60 cm long, predatory salmon, squid, baleen whales, and seabirds. Among all nekton species, pollock has the leading role in the pelagic community; its biomass in the 2000s averaged 8.52 million tonnes wet weight [Ovsyannikov et al., 2013]. The total impact of 30-60 cm pollock on the biota, expressed as prey consumption, is 80.8 million tonnes/year, more than 50% of total nekton prey consumption [Gorbatenko and Levitskaya, 2016]. The principal consumption pressure falls on euphausiids, about 47 million tonnes/year, more than 20% of their gross stock. The mean annual pollock P/B ratio depends on the proportion of young individuals and varies from 0.6 to 0.8 [Dulepova and Sokolovsky, 1988; Shuntov et al., 1993; Dulepova, 2013]. Long-term mean production of this species in the 2000s is estimated at 0.54 million tC/year, equal to 82.2% of total aquatic organism production at trophic level IV (Table 3).
Pelagic squid feeding principally on nekton account for 16.9% of the energy concentrated at trophic level IV (Table 3). Predatory salmon account for about 2.4% of total Okhotsk Sea salmon biomass [Pelagic Macrofauna..., 2012]. Based on organic carbon content, predatory salmon production is 0.002 million tC/year and their share at trophic level IV is 0.3% (Table 3).
Baleen whale biomass in the Okhotsk Sea is estimated at 0.72 million tonnes wet weight (http://www.pices.int/publications/presentations/PICES_14/S3/Miyashita.pdf). The mean baleen whale P/B ratio is taken as 0.01, the same as for whales in Pacific waters around the Kuril Islands [Zavolokin, 2014]; production is 0.001 million tC/year (Table 3). The principal "other" organisms at trophic level IV are seabirds, whose Okhotsk Sea abundance is estimated at 15 million individuals [Shuntov, 2016].
The smallest energy quantity passes through trophic level V in the Okhotsk Sea: 0.015 million tC/year (Table 3). This level is based on fourth-order consumers at the apex of the food pyramid: very large pollock, sharks, and marine mammals (toothed whales and pinnipeds). Long-term mean very large pollock biomass in TINRO-Center surveys during the 2000s was 0.5 million tonnes; shark biomass was 0.1 million tonnes. Toothed whale biomass is estimated at 0.135 million tonnes (http://www.pices.int/publications/presentations/PICES_14/S3/Miyashita.pdf), and pinniped biomass at 0.109 million tonnes (unpublished data of A. E. Kuzin). This group has no rapidly growing forms, and its P/B ratio is only 0.11 (Table 3). For Okhotsk Sea mammals, as for...

## Conclusion
The Okhotsk Sea is characterized by abundant zooplankton development. According to the dominance of animal or plant food in their diets, zooplankton species form a system consisting of two functional elements: "non-predatory" zooplankton, first-order consumers, and "predatory" zooplankton, second- to third-order consumers. The identified trophic groupings are formed principally by these taxa: non-predatory zooplankton, copepods and euphausiids; predatory zooplankton, principally chaetognaths, hyperiids, and jellyfish.
Total zooplankton production for the entire sea in the 2000s, including non-predatory plankton production of 2275 million tonnes and predatory plankton production of 341 million tonnes, is estimated at 2616 million tonnes wet weight.
Predatory zooplankton consumed 16.2% of total annual zooplankton production, with the principal pressure on copepod populations, 19.7% of total annual copepod production. Nekton consumed 6.2% of annual zooplankton production, with maximum pressure on amphipods and euphausiids, whose consumption was one to two orders of magnitude greater than that by predatory zooplankton. Throughout the year, predatory zooplankton and nekton together consumed 22.4% of total annual zooplankton production.
Information accumulated in the 2000s on biomass, production, isotope composition, and organic carbon quantity in groups and components at the major pelagic trophic levels was used to calculate mean values of the principal epipelagic community production parameters. Annual organic matter production was 831.0 million tC at trophic level I, 177.400 at II, 18.100 at III, 0.740 at IV, and 0.016 million tC/year at V. Along the grazing food chain, organic matter is transferred from trophic level I through two principal taxonomic groups of first-order consumers, copepods and euphausiids, at trophic level II. The stronger energy flow, 66%, passes through copepods. At trophic level III the principal energy flow passes through predatory plankton, averaging 96.6%. Among nekton at trophic level III...
@@159
...pollock up to 30 cm predominate, accounting for 1.1% of the total energy passing through that level. At trophic levels IV and V, the principal energy flow passes through differently sized pollock over 30 cm, 82.2 and 61.0%, respectively.
Annual aquatic organism production in the Okhotsk Sea pelagic zone exceeds 1 billion tC (1027.2 million tC), or 17.8 billion tonnes wet weight. Excluding primary production, which accounts for 67.6% of gross production in carbon equivalents, the largest contributions are made by microheterotrophs, 13.3%, and the dominant zooplankton groups: copepods, 11.4%; euphausiids, 5.5%; sagittas, 1.2%; hyperiids, 0.5%. Contributions of functional nekton groups, pollock, herring, squid, salmon, and deep-sea smelt, are approximately one to two orders of magnitude smaller. The heterotrophic portion of Okhotsk Sea plankton communities accounts for about 32% of total production in those communities.
The strong development of representatives of trophic level II, zooplankton, principally copepods and euphausiids, suggests that pressure from planktonic and nektonic predators could be higher. In other words, the Okhotsk Sea food supply could potentially support a larger quantity of nekton than the present level, indicating opportunities to expand pasture aquaculture of valuable pelagic fish, including salmon.
The results contribute to the development of ideas about the patterns of functioning and dynamics of Okhotsk Sea pelagic communities.

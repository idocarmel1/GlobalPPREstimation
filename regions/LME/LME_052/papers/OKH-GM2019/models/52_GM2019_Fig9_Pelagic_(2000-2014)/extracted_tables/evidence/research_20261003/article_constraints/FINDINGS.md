# Independent source and feasibility audit

This is a scientific audit of the existing provisional Figure 9 readings. It creates no balanced model, changes no source values, and does not adopt tentative endpoints or partly hidden labels as verified observations. Original Russian PDFs are authoritative; the new English translation is a reading aid.

## Factors and microbial separation

`conversion_factors_22.csv` and `.json` contain all 22 group slots, the reciprocal carbon fractions, provenance and uncertainty. Nineteen living groups have Table 3 factors; the predatory-fish crosswalk to tier V Other**** remains tentative. In figure order these factors are phytoplankton 20; copepods 14; euphausiids 10.6; hyperiids 14.5; chaetognaths 20; salmon 8.2; squidIII 11.9; herring 7.2; smelt 8.7; small pollock 10.2; capelin 8.8; jellyfish 285.2; medium pollock 11.11; squidIV 11.94; predatory salmon 8.2; baleen whales 5.43; large pollock 11.11; predatory fish 5.43; predatory mammals 5.43. Source: 2019 Table 3, printed p.154 / PDF p.12, visually checked.

Bacteria 9.4 can additionally be recovered as an **explicit supporting-source coefficient** from the dissertation p.60; it is not a separately printed 2019 coefficient. Protozoa remain unresolved: dissertation p.60 reports 27 gC/m² and 270 g wet/m², implying 10, but the accompanying whole-sea total 0.33 billion wet tonnes contradicts 270 g/m² over 1.544 million km², which would yield 416.88 million tonnes. This is a flagged derivation, not a reliable replacement. Detritus has no reported wet/carbon factor or stock; assigning the microbial factor to detritus has no source justification.

Dissertation pp.59–60 does not supply period-matched whole-sea bacterial or protozoan B/PB/QB. It provides local summer bacterial B ranges, and PB 0.2–0.9 adjacent to **daily** production; those are not annual 2000–2014 model inputs. The pooled 2019 row is Bwet 64.0, BC 6.8, PB 20.0 and PC 136.2. Figure 9 splits PC into 101.1+35.1=136.2 but does not split stocks or turnover. Equal PB 20 and equal wet/carbon 9.4 for both would be a transparent **separation assumption**, not recovered source data. With exact figure P and PB 20 it gives BC 5.055 and 1.755, Bwet 47.517 and 16.497, totals 6.81 C and 64.014 wet: these are near the rounded pooled values but not numerically identical. Allocating the source stock in proportion to figure P is another explicit assumption. Neither is selected in this audit. A merged microbial group would lose the bacteria→protozoa internal flow and the requested 22-group network.

The dissertation whole-sea totals 0.95 billion wet tonnes bacteria and 0.33 billion protozoa, divided by 9.4, give 101.064 and 35.106 million tC/year, close to the figure 101.1 and 35.1. This helps explain the construction, but does not demonstrate two independently measured 9.4 conversion factors. Also, source production 67.5 and 27 gC/m²/year over 1.544 million km² yields 104.22 and 41.688 million tC/year, not 101.1 and 35.1. Preserve these source variants.

## What Table 3 P/B means

The source does **not** demonstrate that the coefficients are merely observed stock-growth rates. Methods pp.146–147 explicitly discuss somatic and reproductive growth, exuviae and liquid metabolites; copepod production uses Boysen–Jensen P=B 2−B 1+Be, with Be defined to include grazing, natural mortality and losses during life. This is a production framework that accounts for losses, not just the surviving stock increment. Fish coefficients are borrowed from prior literature: herring.34 from Radchenko 1994; juvenile pollock 2.5 from Radchenko 2015; medium pollock.6–.8 from other studies (pp.155–156). There is no source statement that fish P/B equals total mortality Z or that a closed Ecopath steady state is intended. Do not enter P/B as Z or invent a mortality coefficient.

The sampled/modelled epipelagic boundary is explicit: plankton 0–200 m (p.145); salmon reside for only part of the year; squid ascend from deeper layers to feed at night (p.155). These facts justify considering an open-system migration formulation qualitatively. They do not determine the annual net subsidy. In particular, the herring feeding source 2016 p.190 / PDF p.6 explains spring stock 529 kt versus autumn 2031 kt mainly as survey undercount, spawning aggregations outside trawl coverage, coastal juveniles and ice. It uses an expert **whole-sea** stock 2500 kt after combining evidence. Those seasonal numbers cannot be subtracted to infer BA or import. Pollock feeding source 2016 p.200 / PDF p.7 uses spring northern epipelagic stock, reportedly 96.6% of whole-sea biomass; it supplies no group-specific net migration estimate. Negative EE residual alone is not evidence for growth-only P/B or a measured migration subsidy.

## Carbon currency and exact failures

Carbon currency removes the need for prey wet/carbon factors to reconstruct diets and consumption. It also removes a wet-mass energy failure caused by differing prey/body water content: chaetognaths have carbon R=.8×20.666−12.78=+3.7528, while wet R=.8×287.3996−256.2=−26.28032 million tonnes/year. Carbon should be retained as a distinct physically interpretable diagnostic variant.

It cannot create an exact source-preserving Ecopath solution automatically. Ecopath requires P=B×PB within its selected currency. The printed BC column is rounded and sometimes inconsistent: jellyfish BC.01×PB 10=.1, whereas Table/Figure P=.144; converting source Bwet 4.1/factor 285.2 gives BC.0143758766 and P.143758766. Small pollock printed BC.10 differs materially from.8/10.2=.07843137; the dissertation counterpart prints.08. Preserve the literals and distinguish alternative calculations; do not present a fitted B or P/B as printed source data.

## Which terms can be identified

For each living group let M 2 be predation, Y fisheries mortality/removal, BA net stock accumulation, E net outward migration/export (negative for import), and M 0 the other-mortality **flow**. The production identity is P=M 2+Y+BA+E+M 0. Thus the source diet/Q/P can constrain **the combined sink** Y+BA+E, but cannot separate its terms. Requiring 0≤EE≤1 yields−M 2≤Y+BA+E≤P−M 2. If M 2>P, a negative BA or net inward subsidy can satisfy the production equation; this is a feasibility statement, not an observed amount. Positive catch makes the deficit larger. The paper supplies no per-group annual Y, BA, E or EE values. A source-period mean biomass is not an endpoint pair. Even a reported endpoint change would identify BA only after accounting for the open spatial boundary.

Under the authorized sole-detritus routing and zero detritus imports/exports, BA_D=Σ(GS×Q+M 0)−Q_D. Residual detritus accumulation/depletion is allowed, and no source detritus stock exists to check depletion sustainability. This choice closes the detritus accounting equation, while living-stock and energy failures remain. Changing Y,BA,E changes M 0 and hence the detritus residual; it cannot infer those unknowns. A finite-stock depletion bound such as BA≥−B/15 would additionally assume B is the initial stock and steady linear depletion across 15 years; the paper gives a period mean, so that bound is not source-derived.

`testable_targets_and_identifiability.json` records what each reported result can test and what it cannot identify. `audit_metadata.json` records source hashes and equation conventions. All outputs are confined to this research directory.

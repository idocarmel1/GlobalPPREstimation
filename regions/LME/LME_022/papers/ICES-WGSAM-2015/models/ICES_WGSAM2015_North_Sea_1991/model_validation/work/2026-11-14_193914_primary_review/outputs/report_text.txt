Primary configuration and PPR comparisons

Use ICES North Sea 1991 with separate detritus and EE floor ON at 0.1 as the primary configuration. Retain pooled ICES and corrected East Coast Scotland as sensitivity cases. ICES is already the locally selected model; this report records the analytical choice for researcher review.

Comparison basis: 2019 landings plus discards, using the same 372 mapped catch labels and 2,717,197.286 t of catch. Scotland leaves 12 labels unresolved; their 63.412 t of catch is excluded from both models here and their Scotland PPR remains unknown. Values below are million tonnes carbon per year (Mt C/year).

All-source PPR sums the returned primary-producer (PP), detritus and synthetic-import columns. PP-only is the producer component of the same food-web calculation, not a detritus-free rerun. ICES is higher than Scotland on the all-source measure but lower on PP-only; neither comparison establishes which model is more accurate. Wet-weight requirements are converted to carbon by dividing by 9 once.

Pooling reduces ICES all-source PPR by 8.1% with GE and 0.70% with TE, with PP-only unchanged. It assigns one shared detritus cost to DOM, POM and Discards while retaining all groups, diets, fate routing and internal discard returns. It is a common-cost sensitivity, not a literal merger of the source groups.

The small pooled TE change conceals offsetting changes: the Discards contribution falls by 22.676 Mt C/year, while POM and DOM rise by 11.552 and 10.123 Mt C/year. Separate detritus therefore remains the primary choice: it preserves distinct source-compartment costs without adding an equal-cost assumption.

Turning Scotland’s EE floor ON reduces GE all-source PPR by 0.14%; TE rises by only 2.658 t C/year (0.0000061%). PP-only is unchanged. These small effects do not resolve the models’ structural differences. For the full 384-label ICES mapping, primary GE and TE all-source totals are 81.996 and 142.092 Mt C/year; Scotland has no complete 384-label total.

Evidence and limits of the choice

Geographic fit favors ICES for the whole LME: the declared IVa–IVb–IVc footprint covers approximately 87% of LME_022, compared with Scotland’s 37–42% source-map range. These are boundary interpretations, not statistical intervals or recovered native model grids. Both transfer historical coefficients to 2019 catch: ICES represents 1991 and Scotland 1991–1995.

Source fidelity remains a limitation. The deposited ICES Rpath port materially differs from the accepted native EwE Key Run, whose database was not recovered. Broad geographic coverage and closed numerical budgets do not establish native-model equivalence. Scotland’s narrower shelf and fleet domain also limits its application to all North Sea catches.

All four cases pass strict model mass-balance and SPPR-budget checks and have finite, nonnegative coefficients. ICES nevertheless retains WARN grades: its separate diet-normalization check has a maximum deviation of 8.325 × 10⁻⁵, GE detritus feedback remains high, and TE has five near-zero-efficiency groups. Pooling removes the extreme detritus-cost warning but does not clear the remaining warnings. Scotland’s TE warning also persists.

The EE floor is a runtime mortality-export assumption, coupled with effective detritus stock closure and operational detritus EE = 1; it is not an observed parameter correction. At 0.1 it affects eight ICES living groups, versus only Seabirds, Seals and Cetaceans in Scotland. It does not place a floor on GE or TE. Same-engine Scotland OFF controls exactly reproduce the corrected historical matrices, so that engine-version difference has been checked.

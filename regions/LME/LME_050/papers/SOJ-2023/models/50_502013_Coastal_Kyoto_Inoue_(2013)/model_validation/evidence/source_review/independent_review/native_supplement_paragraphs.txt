Online Resource 1.
Benthos species from sampling
Sampling of the benthic community was conducted in 2013 and 2014 at Tango Bay as a representative of the CAK (Table S1-1) and as part of the Japan Long Term Ecological Research Network (JaLTER) cruise onboard the R.V. Ryokuyo-maru (Kyoto University, 8 t). Organisms were collected at depths of 5, 10, 30, 60, 100, 150, and 200 m using a NRIFE II type beam trawl net (height: 20 cm, width: 2 m, mesh size: 5 mm; Kimoto et al. 2001). The research vessel was supposed to trawl three times at each location, with each passage lasting for five minutes at a speed of two knots. However, for some sites, it was not possible to sample three times due to sea conditions (Table S1-1). The towing distance was calculated based on the recorded latitude and longitude positions using a global positioning system (GPS) plotter (HE-61GPII; Honda Electronics Co., Ltd., Aichi, Japan). The towing distance was then used to calculate the sampled surface area (i.e., SSA (km2) = towing distance × beam trawl net width). The organisms were transported to the laboratory on ice and identified for the species level, weighed, and fixed with 70% ethanol. The bottom water temperature and salinity were measured using a conductivity, temperature, and depth profiler (Compact-CTD ASTD687; JFE Advantech Co., Ltd., Nishinomiya, Japan) immediately after net towing.

Benthos species selection for Ecopath
Nine functional groups were selected for the benthic community (Table S1-2). Species selection was mainly based on species dominance in the CAK (i.e., high biomass in benthic sampling). Species were also selected if there were known prey for fishery-targeted fish species in the CAK. Species smaller than 5 mm were not selected in the present study because the net mesh size was 5 mm. Although jellyfish species (e.g., Aurelia coerulea and Nemopilema nomurai) were collected in the CAK, they were not selected in this study because the net substantially affected their structural integrity and no exploitable data on these species in the CAK were available in the literature. The data on the benthic community can help characterize the spatio-temporal variability of coastal biological productivity across depths and seasons (Yokoyama and Hayashi 1980b) and for building an ecosystem-based model.

　P/B is defined to be similar to total mortality (Z) (Allen 1971). In addition, Z is defined to be the same as fishing mortality (F) and natural mortality (M) (Pauly 1980). F was calculated from the catch and biomass (F = Catch / B). Q/B ratios and M were calculated using the life history tool of FishBase (https://www.fishbase.de, “Accessed 3 April 2021”) (Palomares and Pauly 1989; Pauly 1989; Palomares 1991). For invertebrates, M and Q were calculated from the SeaLifeBase (http://www.sealifebase.org, “Accessed 3 April 2021”).

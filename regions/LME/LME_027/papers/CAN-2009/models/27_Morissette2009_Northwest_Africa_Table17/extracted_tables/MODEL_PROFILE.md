# Source model profile

The final model is Table 17, with diets in Table 18, in the 2009 Northwest Africa chapter. It describes the late 1980s across a 3,561,029 km² shelf-and-ocean area bounded by Morocco, Mauritania, Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, the Canary Islands, Gambia, Cape Verde and Western Sahara (printed pp. 6–7). Geographic inclusion of Canary Current LME is stated, but study-area and LME boundaries are not identical. No overlap percentage is inferred here. Catches are a separate 1987–2004 series and partial 1990s mean prose values.

There are no explicit juvenile/adult stanza divisions or spatial-prefix groups. The paper states that most groups used adult diet data because most available biomass estimates were spawning-stock or adult biomass (printed p. 13). This is an evidence limitation, not an exhaustive adult-only taxonomic exclusion rule. Groups reflect trophic guilds/habitats; source membership is Table 3, printed pp. 14–16 / PDF pp. 18–20.

The final `Baleen whales` label therefore represents the source Blue-whale group, not all baleen whales combined. Final `Bathydeersal predators` is the printed spelling for Bathydemersal predators in the membership/prose discussion. Final `Coastal demersal` corresponds to Table 3/prose `Coastal demersals`.

Tables 1–2 use header numbers 10–21 above catches from Large pelagics through Benthos. Join by names to final IDs 12–23; do not join these obsolete numbers directly. Official EcoBase 118 uses another whale sequence: final IDs 6,7,8,9 map to native IDs 8,9,6,7. Full source-native comparison is retained separately.

Groups 24 Benthic producers and 26 Phytoplankton are primary producers; group 27 Detritus is the sole detritus pool. Group 25 Zooplankton includes copepods, hydrozoans, scyphozoans, fish larvae and eggs in Table 3; it is not unambiguously “small zooplankton” for an assimilation convention. Quantitative detritus routing is unreported. Paper-only consumer 25 diet is unknown even though its TL is printed as 2.00.

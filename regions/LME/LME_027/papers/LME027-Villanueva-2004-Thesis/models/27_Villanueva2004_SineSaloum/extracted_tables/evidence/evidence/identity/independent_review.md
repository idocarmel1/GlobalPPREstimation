# Independent source identity and geographic review

The preferred source is Maria Concepcion S. Villanueva’s doctoral thesis, **Biodiversité et relations trophiques dans quelques milieux estuariens et lagunaires de l’Afrique de l’Ouest : adaptations aux pressions environnementales**, Institut National Polytechnique de Toulouse (INP/ENSAT), 2004. The title page states defense on **29 October 2004**; a harvested archive description lists 20 October, which is not substituted for the source date. The original 272-page PDF was downloaded from [the official Archimer PDF](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00180/29122/29305.pdf), checked with PyMuPDF, and copied unchanged into the separate project paper folder `LME027-Villanueva-2004-Thesis`. Metadata and README retain the source URL, bytes, SHA-256, date and role. [National thesis record](https://theses.fr/2004INPT012A). No DOI was established.

The preferred **37-group Sine-Saloum model represents 1991–1992**: Table 6.5, printed p.114/PDF p.138, and the independently recovered 2005 follow-up’s methods and Table 1 explicitly date it. The thesis’s experimental fish sampling in chapter 3 is **1992–1993**, a separate input period. The model’s two-year label is not collapsed into an invented single baseline year. Transferring fixed coefficients to the 2019 reference year would extend the nominal state by 27–28 years; applying them to 1950–2019 would not reconstruct observed food-web change.

The originally supplied 38-group chapter remains separate. It has 24 fish, 10 invertebrate, three producer and one detritus groups; the thesis model has 29 fish, five invertebrate, two producer and one detritus groups. The chapter reports fish biomass 37.7 t/km² versus 9.1 t/km² in the related 37-group follow-up. Chapter/thesis Scomberomorus tritor biomass is 0.004/0.049 t/km²; its P/B is 2.520/0.650 and Q/B is 12.60/6.821 per year. Ethmalosa biomass is 17.657/0.630 and Sardinella maderensis is 0.102/2.413 t/km². These differences establish that the thesis diet cannot silently complete the earlier chapter. The chapter’s source identity is Villanueva, Tito-de-Morais, Weigel & Moreau, pp.405–414 in Chavance et al. (eds.), *Pêcheries maritimes, écosystèmes & sociétés en Afrique de l’Ouest : un demi-siècle de changement*, ACP-UE fisheries research report 15, volume 1. Its cover says October 2004; its legal imprint and copyright say 2005; the symposium was 24–28 June 2002. No explicit chapter model baseline year was found.

The [EcoBase 612 catalogue card](https://ecobase.ecopath.org/php/protect/base_model.php?action=base&ident=&lang=&model=612&pass=&provenance=web) lists Villanueva_1991_SineSaloumEstuary, 1991–1992, 543 km², zero available groups and fleets, with the thesis reference. The [documented official input endpoint](https://ecobase.ecopath.org/php/webser/soap-client.php?no_model=612) returned a 94-byte XML response: “Datas for this model are not available.” Both responses are retained. No native numerical match to the thesis is possible from catalogue metadata. The catalogue’s 543 km² is not used to overwrite the thesis’s 546 km².

The recovered 2005 follow-up, [*Complexity in trophic structure and stability in a modelled West African estuary*](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00000/2554/2171.pdf), confirms the 37-group period and substantial matching biomass pattern, but supplies no full corrected diet matrix. Its group 1 name Sphyraena afra differs from thesis Table 6.5’s representative Sphyraena guanchancho; both occur in the thesis’s Sphyraenidae membership pool. It is supporting lineage evidence, not a silently interchangeable complete export.

The preferred Sine-Saloum model is distinct from the retained broad Northwest Africa 27-group EcoBase 118 model and its Morissette 2009 representation, and from the 51-group Guénette 2014 Mauritanian shelf/Banc d’Arguin models. The latter’s Base/M30/P30 are scenarios of one modeled ecosystem. Distinct model identity does not demonstrate statistically independent field observations: shared West African surveys, literature and ecological proxies remain possible. The central inventory additionally lists archived candidate `27_118_Northwest_Africa_(1987)_1883ddd3`; its listed local model path is absent. This unresolved archived representation is not relabeled as a Villanueva model, nor is its coverage metadata treated as identity evidence. The current selected model remains `27_118_Northwest_Africa_(1987)` with its original signed disqualification and active values unchanged.

## Source consistency gate

Table 6.5 group 20 and the 2005 follow-up name **Epinephelus aeneus***. Annex II.A row 20 names **Hemichromis fasciatus**. Independent rendering of thesis Table 3.3 (printed pp.58–59/PDF pp.82–83) shows a bottom-predator group represented by Epinephelus, with Lutjanus goreensis as a Saloum member, while Hemichromis is separately named. These are not synonyms. A numbered row correspondence does not establish compatible species/group identity; a corrected matrix or author export is still needed before claiming an exact admitted source model.

Group 27’s parameter name **Liza grandisquamis*** and diet name **Liza falcipinnis** have a narrower interpretation: Table 3.3 explicitly includes both in the Saloum **Mugilidés** pool, with L. grandisquamis as representative. That supports a pooled-guild crosswalk while preserving the representative-label disagreement. It does not resolve group 20 or authorize a numerical correction.

## Geographic assessment

The thesis topography and Table 2.1 (printed p.17/PDF p.41) report **546 km²**. Figure 2.2 (printed p.18/PDF p.42) depicts the Saloum, Diomboss and Bandiala arms, north arrow and a 10 km scale. It lacks calibrated coordinate anchors and an unambiguous outer model water boundary. Printed bounds 13°35′–14°10′N and 16°50′–17°W describe a western strip rather than the full eastward estuary depicted. This conflict is retained; the bounding rectangle and the map frame are not treated as the water polygon. The original chapter separately reports 543 km² and a biosphere reserve locator, which also is not substituted for the thesis model extent.

The exact project LME 027 polygon was read from `common_reference_data/geography/LMEs.geojson` and has WGS84 geodesic area **1,123,611.7995273052 km²**, independently agreeing with retained regional geography evidence. For A = intersection/target and B = intersection/study:

- **A ≤ 0.049%**: the unrounded upper bound is 100 × 546 / 1,123,611.7995273052 = **0.048593295320474385%**. It assumes the entire reported study area could be inside the target, so it is a mathematical area cap, not a measured overlap.
- **B is not determined**: the project polygon contains several main estuary channels, but an exact compatible study-water boundary is unavailable. Sample location-point containment does not measure whole-estuary containment. A rectangle or coarse land mask would not recover missing estuary channels and modeled bolongs. Even B = 100% would leave A below 0.05%.

The estuarine, hypersaline nursery system and its local fisheries provide poor geographic/ecological grounds for a whole Canary Current LME replacement without additional representativeness evidence. A small local model can be useful for its own domain; geographic adjacency and shared taxa do not establish regional trophic coefficients. Source and target figures plus calculations are retained under `../geography/`; rendered images were inspected for legibility and source interpretation.

## Bounded recovery outcome

The main chapter, official author thesis, official related follow-up, EcoBase catalogue and its documented native input endpoint were inspected. Exact title/missing-diet searches, repository searches and specific group-name conflict searches located no compatible 38-group native export and no corrected 37-group matrix. A later Bamboung/MPA matrix is a different modeled domain and version; its cited use of earlier Saloum information is not a compatible replacement. The recovery result is **downloaded primary thesis tables with unresolved internal source conflict**, rather than evidence that no better data exist anywhere. Authors were not contacted.

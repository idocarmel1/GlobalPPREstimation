# Sine-Saloum thesis model, 1991-1992

Preferred source: Maria Concepcion Villanueva (2004), downloaded thesis, Table 6.5 (printed p. 114; PDF p. 138), with Annex II.A (printed pp. 211-212; PDF pp. 235-236). The original chapter is a separate, retained 38-group variant.

The thesis model has 37 compartments: 29 fish groups, five invertebrate consumer groups (Crevettes, Crabes, Macrobenthos, Meiobenthos and Zooplancton), two producers and one detritus pool. It represents the Sine-Saloum estuary, Senegal; it is not evidence of whole-LME representativeness. The preferred thesis reports 546 km2 (printed p. 17 / PDF p. 41); the separate chapter reports 543 km2. All tabulated B and Y values use wet weight and annual rates; habitat fractions are not tabulated. The import metadata identifies whole-model-area biomass, while retaining unknown habitat fractions. No second area conversion was applied.

Table 6.5 states that starred fish names are principal species chosen to represent pooled groups; unstarred fish names denote single-species groups. Table 3.3 (PDF pp. 82-83; printed pp. 58-59) gives grouped species and Saloum membership indicators. Its descriptions and Table 6.5 are not perfectly consistent: Pseudotolithus representative names differ; some Table 3.3 pools appear unstarred in Table 6.5; and a Psettodidae-labelled block contains Chloroscombrus. Source spelling and grouping conflicts are retained; no silent source correction was applied.

The methods (PDF pp. 76-81) define shrimp, crab and benthic pools. Shrimp membership includes Penaeus duorarum, P. notialis and Macrobrachium in the wider study; Penaeus duorarum is the chosen Sine-Saloum representative. Crabs are Portunidae, notably Callinectes amnicola and C. pallidus. Macrobenthos mainly includes molluscs, with bivalves, gastropods, polychaetes and amphipods discussed in the parameter methods. Corbula trigona is a general biological representative, while Table 3.3 marks Anadara senilis for Saloum. Neither representative is an exhaustive species list. The thesis does not establish a cephalopod compartment. No life-stage or multistanza subdivision is specified in this model.

The diet matrix is explicitly prey rows by numbered predator columns. It contains 37 prey and 34 consumers across two continued blocks, columns 1-17 and 18-34. Although the caption says percent, the source uses decimal fractions summing to 0.999-1.000. Exact values, blanks and source names are preserved. No normalization or division by 100 was applied.

Group 27 can be reconciled as the documented Mugilidés pool: Table 6.5 uses Liza grandisquamis as principal, while the annex names Liza falcipinnis; both are explicit members in Table 3.3. Group 20 cannot currently be reconciled: Table 6.5 gives Epinephelus aeneus, while the annex gives Hemichromis fasciatus; Table 3.3 lists them in separate compartments. All canonical cells involving group 20 are withheld as unknown. The complete numbered source matrix is preserved in the literal evidence.

Source admission is BLOCKED. No Ecopath/SpPR calculator was loaded. GS, BA, routing, migration, habitat fractions, imports and separate discards remain unknown. Printed Y dashes remain unknown; group 22 Y = 0.000 is an explicit printed zero. The eight import files are partial evidence schemas, accompanied by a loss ledger and exact source companions, not a ready model for calculation or adoption.

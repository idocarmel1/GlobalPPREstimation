# Independent High/Medium membership reassessment — 30 September 2026

All 246 assigned records were reassessed: 130 previously High and 116 previously Medium. The resulting proposal contains **184 High, 37 Medium and 25 Low** decisions: **54 raises, 167 retentions and 25 reductions**. All 130 previous High records remain High after checking their actual source boundaries. This is a confidence review of the current retained assignments. Candidate group changes are explicitly marked `not_adopted`; no mapping, weight, coefficient, solver result or canonical artifact was changed by this review.

The companion `high_medium_review.json` contains a separate decision, current group IDs, rule, rationale, evidence, contradictions and candidate correction for every record. The updated template rules and group-taxonomy guidance governed the decisions. Weight confidence is outside this sub-review and was checked separately by the parent review.

## Evidence and limits

The exact retained Appendix 6.1 group definitions, printed pp.322–341, were read together with competing groups. An authoritative WoRMS lookup was attempted for all 246 names. Of those, 244 returned taxonomic records; the non-taxonomic “Miscellaneous marine crustaceans” label and the spelling “Charybdis feriatus” did not return a usable exact record. The latter remains a crab by its supported genus identity; the failed lookup is not represented as a verified synonym.

Full FishBase species HTML was retrieved for all 78 binomial taxa in the previously Medium set, including Environment, maximum size and length convention, and Biology. Eleven genus inventories were also checked: Stolephorus, Thryssa, Dussumieria, Upeneus, Cypselurus, Spratelloides, Hippocampus, Dasyatis, Himantura, Alopias and Isurus. Additional authoritative sources and selected taxon accounts were consulted for conflicts and regional relevance. The JSON labels whether a source was a full retained definition, retrieved taxon/species record, full genus inventory, or an indexed excerpt/selected account. In particular, external PDF excerpts are not claimed to be complete PDF audits. Retrieval date is 2026-09-30.

Taxonomic membership, current ecological descriptions and the model's historical taxonomic concept are separate checks. The records retain the actual retrieved taxonomic classification. Broad catch categories are assessed as reporting concepts as well as taxonomic labels: reporting classifiers corroborate an interpretation, but do not independently prove every species exclusion. A relevant counterexample flags incomplete support; it does not measure its contribution to regional catch.

## Strongest revisions

- Explicit and exhaustive unions now qualify as High membership: Sciaenidae across its three croaker groups; Elasmobranchii, Carcharhinus, Carcharhinidae and Batoidea across the complete demersal/pelagic elasmobranch partition; Mollusca across cephalopod and non-cephalopod groups. Unknown composition still limits allocation weights.
- Many species previously assigned an ecological Medium satisfy every generic criterion and now qualify for M3 High. These include clearly pelagic fish with maxima above 30 cm, several small pelagics with verified total-length maxima, and sharks with an unambiguous documented habitat fit. Larimichthys polyactis belongs in the large-croaker pair despite its common name “small yellow croaker.” Isurus is High after checking both species in the genus.
- Hilsa kelee falls to Low because its recorded maximum is 35 cm TL while the retained small-pelagic group requires maximum TL at or below 30 cm. Clupanodon thrissa and Amblygaster sirm also fall to Low, but for unresolved near-boundary SL-to-TL interpretation rather than a verified TL contradiction.
- Upeneus, Polynemidae, Acanthuridae/Acanthurus, Gobiidae and Exocoetidae have material size-scope gaps in the retained small-fish assignments. The flyingfish example is especially direct: Cheilopogon unicolor, separately recorded in the same catch universe, reaches 38 cm TL. Genus/family review cannot simply inherit a smaller representative species' result.
- Decapterus russelli and Megalops cyprinoides have documented benthopelagic ecology that competes with the retained large-pelagic pair. Elops machnata has conflicting Environment and Biology fields. The selected model has one generic Benthopelagic fish group (27); the appendix distinguishes small and large benthopelagic fish, and the final merger scope remains unverified. Several carangids have adult reef/bottom ecology or conflicting authoritative habitat accounts. Their Low ratings concern the actual retained assignment; the wider candidate sets are hypotheses for later adjudication.
- Dasyatidae includes the pelagic stingray Pteroplatytrygon violacea, whose published distribution includes the South China Sea. The “Large rays” reporting classifier does not document its exclusion. Engraulidae likewise retains a specific scope uncertainty concerning larger anchovies; this is not a claim that those fish contribute a measured share of the catch label “Anchovies, round herrings.” Sphyrnidae's regional winghead component prevents unconditional pelagic exclusivity.

## Negative findings and reusable lessons

1. **No blanket penalty for broadness or unnamed membership.** A source-defined family pool or exhaustive group union can have High membership even when its allocation weights remain uncertain. M3 depends on complete criteria, not whether every species is enumerated.
2. **Representative lists remain representative.** The two prominent Loligo species do not restrict Cephalopods to that genus. Mortality examples for shrimps do not restrict Shrimps to those taxa. Parameter donors do not redefine group membership.
3. **Honor dedicated pools and exclusions.** Acetes is explicitly assigned to Zooplankton; it cannot be moved into Shrimps merely because it is a shrimp. True crabs and shrimps are excluded from residual benthic crustaceans. Pennahia is an explicit small-croaker source exception. Pomfret survey categories are not permission to merge all similarly named fishes.
4. **Historical taxonomy needs a documented bridge.** Current Harpadontidae placement of Saurida/Harpadon does not itself overturn the source Synodontidae concept. The source's explicit Saurida examples and the historical classification evidence resolve that boundary. Likewise the grouper source concept and historical FAO classification explain modern Epinephelidae versus Serranidae. These are concept checks, not claims that current authorities use the old hierarchy.
5. **Stored display labels are not source criteria.** The stored “Adult groupers” name does not create an age cutoff absent from the source definition. Conversely, explicit hairtail, croaker and generic large-fish stanzas determine the eligible pair; unobserved age composition affects its weights.
6. **Length convention matters.** Maximum length, not common length, drives the stated fish-size boundary. An SL/FL maximum above 30 cm establishes a size above 30 cm TL; a value below 30 cm SL/FL does not establish the reverse. Far-below-boundary records can support Medium while unresolved near-boundary records deserve Low. The first Rastrelliger kanagurta record omitted a convention; its separately reported female maximum of 42.1 cm TL supplies the required evidence.
7. **Read biology past coarse habitat tags.** “Reef-associated” alone neither proves demersal habitat nor rules out pelagic activity. Adult and juvenile habitat statements can differ. Where the detailed account resolves the operational boundary, raise confidence; where it conflicts or leaves an equally plausible dedicated habitat pool, preserve that uncertainty.
8. **Use regionally relevant exceptions carefully.** A remote freshwater Thryssa or an out-of-region giant seahorse is not automatic proof that the regional catch assignment is wrong. The Dasyatidae and Gobiidae concerns have regional support; category exclusions and local catch composition remain unverified.
9. **Do not mechanically resolve ambiguous taxon records.** Accepted records and synonyms/homonyms were inspected rather than accepting the first lookup result. Ibacus and Holothuriidae returned multiple records; Teuthida's nomenclatural status does not remove it from Cephalopoda.
10. **Keep regional transfer separate.** A broader geographic range or oceanic occurrence is not, by itself, a membership downgrade when the source generic guild fits. Regional model representativeness and weight transfer remain separate concerns. No confidence was raised merely to improve the High percentage.

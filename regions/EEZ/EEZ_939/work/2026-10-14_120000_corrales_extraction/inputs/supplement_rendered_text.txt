The pre-balancing analysis (PREBAL) was designed to assess the data quality before 
the mass balance process and/or dynamic simulations are developed with Ecopath and 
Ecosim food web models in order to ensure that the input data are reasonable and robust 
and to indirectly verify model accuracy and robustness (Link, 2010 ). Therefore, once 
the input data has been collected and analysed, it is needed to test the model through the 
PREBAL analysis (Heymans et al., 2016). 
Link (2010 ) describes a set of diagnostics that can be tested th ought graphical 
representation, including biomass and vital rates vital rates across taxa and trophic 
levels, biomass and vital rates ratios, and total production and removals. 
In this study , we checked the diagnostics related to  biomass and vital rates (P/B and  
Q/B) across taxa and trophic levels. Biomass and vital rates of each functional group 
were plotted  along the x -axis in  order of  decreasing trophic level. In the biomass 
diagnostic, we check the two rules that Link (2010) proposed: 1) the range of biomass 
should span 5 -7 orders of magnitude and 2) the slope should decline by 5 -10% with 
increasing trophic level across all the taxa.  In addition, biomass and vital rates of 
predators should be lower than  prey. For all of these diagnostics, groups with values 
that greatly exceed or fall below the general trend were examined.  
Results of the PREBAL analysis in both time periods are shown in Fig. 1  (1990s) and 
Fig. 2 (2010s). Both models complied with the two rules that we checked for biomass as 
the range of biomass spanned 5 orders of magnitude and the slope declined by 5.5% and 
6.1% in 1990s and 2010s time periods, respectively.  
The overall diagnostics applied to our models showed general coherent reduction of 
parameters values with increasing trophic levels (Fig. 1 and 2). Regarding biomass, i n 
2010s period all the functional groups were represented, while in the 1990s period, new 
alien demersal fishes ( functional group or F.G. 24) and alien medium pelagic fishes 
(F.G. 35) were not present because they  did not invaded the ecosystem  yet (Fig. 1). In 
both models, sea birds ( F.G. 38) presented low biomass despite their trophic position  
(Fig. 1a and 2a).  This is due to their uncertainty of biomass estimates and their trophic 
positions in Ecopath models, as an important part of their  diet are discards, which EwE 
considers as a detritus group with TL = 1.  

PAGE 15
The results also showed  that phytoplankton (F.G.1), micro and mesozooplankton ( F.G. 
3), polychaetes (F.G. 6), benthic invertebrates (F.G. 12), small pelagic fishes ( F.G. 31), 
small native demersal fishes (F.G. 20), native medium pelagic fishes (F.G. 34) and large 
pelagic fishes ( F.G. 36) could potentially be overestimated, while benthic primary 
producers (F.G. 2), alien herbivorous (F.G. 22), demersal fishes in the upper slope (F.G. 
26) and dolphins could be underestimated  in both time periods  (Fig. 1a and 2a). As 
expected by Link (2010), groups at the two extremes (in the ICS models , phytoplankton 
(F.G. 1) and dolphins (F.G. 39)) tend to present out of trend biomass values. In the case 
of small native demersal fishes (F.G. 20), we can justify their higher biomass due to the 
large number of species included in this group. For native medium pelagic fishes  (F.G. 
34) and large pelagic fishes (F.G. 36), the last one with biomass estimate based on stock 
assessment, we could expect lower biomass but the requirements of the model impose d 
higher biomass mainly due to their large catch, as their predation mortal ities showed 
low values (Table 2  in the manuscript). For the remainder groups, biomass estimates 
were calculated by the model, showing the need of stock assessment for these groups. In 
absence of biomass estimates, adjustments on the diet matrix were needed in order to 
ensure the correct diagnostic in PREBAL. 
Moreover, in the 1990 s period, large native demersal fishes ( F.G. 21) presented a high 
biomass. We could assume that their biomass estimate was overestimate d but, as in 
other cases, the large catch impose d high requirements to the model, as their predation 
mortalities showed low values (Table 1 in the manuscript). This group did not show a 
high biomass in the 2010s period due to its overexploitation during the period. 
Benthic primary producers ( F.G. 2) and alien herbivorous ( F.G. 22) presented low  
biomass values as these groups live exclusively in the shallow waters, while the model 
included depths from 0 to 20 0 m. Therefore, we could expect low biomasses for  these 
groups. Demersal fishes in the upper slope ( F.G. 26) also showed low biomass 
estimates. These could be related to the lower number of hauls in these strata on the 
trawl survey used to es timate biomasses and/or to the deep-sea faunal and biomass  
impoverishment of the Eastern Mediterranean Sea compared to the western basin 
(Goren and Galil, 2002).  
In addition, alien crabs ( F.G. 11) and nat ive crabs ( F.G. 10) showed low  biomass in 
1990s and 2010s time periods,  respectively (Fig. 1a and 2a). We could expect these 

PAGE 16
results as the biomass of alien crabs (F.G. 11) increased during the periods while the 
biomass of native crabs decreased although the native crabs  (F.G. 10) did not present 
high values in 1990s period. 
Finally, mullets ( F.G. 15) and hake ( F.G. 17) showed low  biomass estimates  in the 
2010s period as a result of overexploitation and niche displacement to deeper water  by 
their alien competitors (goatfishes (F.G. 16) and alien lizardfish (F.G. 25)) (Galil and 
Zenetos, 2002; Rilov and Galil, 2009).  
In the ICS model, P/B ratios for alien herbivorous (F.G. 22), benthic invertebrates (F.G. 
12), turtles ( F.G. 37) and dolphins ( F.G. 39) were low, while those for phytoplankton 
(F.G. 1), micro and mesozooplankton ( F.G. 3), macrozooplankton ( F.G. 4), gelatinous 
plankton (F.G. 5) and benthopelagic cephalopods (F.G. 14) were high (Fig. 1b and 2b). 
Initial P/B values for macrozooplankton (F.G. 4), polychaetes (F.G. 6) and suprabenthos 
(F.G. 7) were modified as we were able to find better parameters estimates taking into 
account the PREBAL analysis . For macrozooplankton  (F.G. 4), initial P/B value was 
22.92 (from Labat and Cuzin-Roudy (1996)), and as this  value was high we decided to 
apply a P/Q of 0.3 taking into account the P/Q of 0.33 for micro and mesozoopla nkton 
(F.G. 3). For polychaetes  (F.G. 6) and suprabenthos (F.G. 7), initial P/B value s were 
weighted values of different species but they were low. Therefore, we decided to use the 
maximum value. For t he other problematic groups, in absence of better parameter 
estimates, we decided to use common values in other Ecopath models set up in the 
Mediterranean Sea (Coll et al., 2006 ; Coll et al., 2007; Tsagarakis et al., 2010; Corrales 
et al., 2015). 
Finally, in the ICS model, Q/B ratios for alien herbivorous ( F.G. 22), benthic 
invertebrates ( F.G. 12), shrimps ( F.G. 8 and 9), crabs ( F.G. 10 and 11), rocky fishes 
(F.G. 19) and turtles ( F.G. 37) were low, while those for micro and mesozooplankton 
(F.G. 3), macrozooplankton ( F.G. 4), gelatinous plankton ( F.G. 5), suprabenthos ( F.G. 
7), benthopelagic cephalopods ( F.G. 14), sea birds ( F.G. 38) and dolphins were high. 
Q/B ratios for shrimps  (F.G. 8 and 9),  crabs (F.G. 10 and 11), rocky fishes (F.G. 19), 
seabirds (F.G. 38) and dolphins (F.G. 39) were maintained as they came from empirical 
equations (See Supplement Online Information section 1) . Initial Q/B ratios for alien 
herbivorous (F.G. 22) and demersal sharks ( F.G. 29) were modified as those had low 
and high P/Q ratios respectively. For alien herbivorous (F.G. 22) we decided to apply a 

PAGE 17
high P/Q ratio (0.25) and for demersal sharks  (F.G. 29) a low P/Q (0.15), taking into 
account their trophic position and ecological principles. The other problematic groups, 
in absence of better parameter estimates and as for P/B ratios, we decided to use 
common values in other Ecopath models set up in the Mediterranean Sea (Coll et al., 
2006; Coll et al., 2007; Tsagarakis et al., 2010; Corrales et al., 2015). 
To conclude, the PREBAL analysis applied to the ICS model showed that in general the 
models complied general ecological principles. Moreover, it showed that for some 
groups, better information is required, mainly for planktonic groups , which should 
inform future scientific research objectives in the area . As new information is being 
generated, the models could be updated and therefore, its quality and reliability 
increased. In addition, with the calibration of the 1990s model to available historical 
data, some of these parameters should be rechecked and the refore data input of the 
model may improve.

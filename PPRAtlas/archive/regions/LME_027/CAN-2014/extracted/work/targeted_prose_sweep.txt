Ecopath models account for the biomass of each functional group of species, their diet composition, production per unit of biomass (P/B), consumption per unit of biomass (Q/B), mortality from predators and fishing, accumulation of biomass and net migration. The principle behind this ecosystem modelling approach is that, on a yearly basis, biomass and energy are conserved. The proportion of the mortality of each group that is accounted by the model (fishing, predation, biomass accumulation, migration) is called ecotrophic efficiency (EE). The P/B is considered equal to total mortality under equilibrium condition [4] since production and losses would be equal. The total mortality (Z) is thus computed as the sum of fishing mortality (F) and natural mortality (M) which includes mortality by predation.
